# Sources, assumptions and market boundaries

Research date: 12 October 2026. This package visualizes the supplied research framework and selected anchors from the preceding primary-source research. Historical industry numbers are consultant or company-cited estimates, rather than administrative census measurements.

## Stage table and funnel

The supplied stage table uses virtual designs ~1,000 before AI versus 100,000–1 billion in the AI scenario; physical candidates ~1,000 versus 1,000–10,000; wet-lab profiling ~50 versus an illustrative 50→500 expansion; clinical entry 1 versus 1; expected approvals ~0.14 versus unknown. These are user-supplied assumptions, not empirical cohort statistics.

The denominator is one clinical entrant. The second reference image uses a different denominator, per approved drug; its counts are not mixed into this table. ~0.14 is an illustrative expected count, not an independently validated success probability. DNA-encoded libraries are not equivalent to fully characterized discrete candidates. Virtual design has costs and quality constraints. The diagram states a working capacity-gate hypothesis, not that design quality or clinical biology cease to matter.

## Supporting figure 02: consistent-scope LST comparison

The ACROBiosystems HKEX filing of 30 Sep 2026 reports 2025 global LST US$41.5bn and China US$7.7bn under its F&S scope, printed p.65 / PDF p.74. Research biological reagents are global US$26.4bn and China US$4.6bn, printed p.67 / PDF p.76. The residual other-tool values are derived: 41.5−26.4 =15.1 and 7.7−4.6 =3.1, in US$bn. China/global share is 7.7÷41.5 =18.6% rounded. This scope does not reconcile automatically to broader laboratory or manufacturing markets. ## Illustrative and qualitative figures

Figure 03 uses the supplied customer mixes: reagents 30/25/15/10/20%, consumables 20/20/15/30/15%, and instruments 25/20/10/15/30%, for academia, biotech/pharma R&D, CRO/CDMO, pharma-owned production and IVD development. These are illustrative purchasing shares. Hospitals are not independently modeled, and distributors are not an end-customer category.

Figure 06 compares 100,000 tests at 100µL in 96-well plates with 1,000,000 tests at 5µL in 1,536-well plates. Reagents fall from 10L to 5L; full-plate counts are ceiling(100,000/96)=1,042 and ceiling(1,000,000/1,536)=652. Tips per test are assumed to fall from 1 to 0.1, giving 100,000 tips in both cases. Volume index 1,000; reagent index 50; plate index 62.6; tip index 100, against a baseline of 100. Dead volume, controls, retests, ASP and service spending are excluded. The two panels use different y-axis scales.

Figure 07 uses illustrative cumulative funding pools of US$50bn, US$100bn and US$200bn. No time horizon is specified. Allocations are 30% research equipment/automation, 25% research reagents, 20% research consumables, 15% manufacturing bioprocess tools and 10% services/other. Research-use and manufacturing-use allocations are mutually exclusive. These are not realized financing, annual TAM or a company revenue forecast.

Figures 05, 08 and 09 use qualitative analyst positions. Bubble area in figure 05 is schematic relative opportunity, not measured TAM. Axis positions and the gold emphasis on ACROBiosystems and NanoMicro are not numerical scores, stock recommendations, market-share estimates or measured AI revenue. Supply barriers do not establish a current shortage.

Figures 04 and 10 describe a conditional mechanism: funded projects → physical experimentation → net tool consumption and purchasing → company share → profit and cash flow. AI funding announcements alone do not establish LST orders. Miniaturization, faster candidate triage, resin reuse and pricing can offset higher experiment counts.
