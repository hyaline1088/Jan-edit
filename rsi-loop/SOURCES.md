# RSI: AI design and biological validation

Figure 16. English conceptual diagram. Prepared 13 October 2026.

## What the diagram means

The user confirmed RSI means **recursive self-improvement**. The figure illustrates a possible biology-grounded improvement loop: an AI model or experimental policy designs experiments; physical construction and biological assays generate evidence; data are curated; the model and/or selection policy are updated and benchmarked before the next cycle. The intended recursive layer improves the system that chooses future experiments, not just the biological candidate.

This is a conceptual RSI mechanism, not a claim that autonomous, sustained or exponential RSI has been established in biological R&D. Closing a design–build–test–learn loop is not by itself evidence of RSI. A system may simply reuse a fixed model with new context or improve a biological candidate without improving the model's persistent capability. Those cases need to be distinguished from validated changes to the model or learning policy.

Experiments can inform candidate ranking, model learning, selection policies and the choice of the next assay. Promotion of a new model/policy requires controlled evaluation, with independent or held-out experimental evidence, reproducibility and tests of generalization. No specific improvement rate, success probability, cost saving, candidate count or AI revenue is estimated.

The LST rail maps the loop to construction, measurement and connected laboratory workflows. The procurement expression is an analyst framework for recurring inputs, not a forecast. Actual purchases depend on funded activity, assay selection, miniaturization, reuse and price; instrument capital expenditure and services require separate accounting.

## Primary sources

1. [Self-driving laboratories to autonomously navigate the protein fitness landscape — Nature Chemical Engineering](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44286-023-00002-4). The reported system connects designed protein sequences to automated construction/expression/characterization and uses the resulting evidence to inform the next experimental round. This supports biological closed-loop learning, not unlimited recursive AI capability growth. The publisher's indexed abstract/figure description was accessible; full-page retrieval was blocked by its identity redirect during this request.
2. [A generalized platform for artificial intelligence-powered autonomous enzyme engineering — Nature Communications, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-61209-y). A reported platform connects ML/LLM capabilities to biofoundry automation. Used as a second example of autonomous experimentation, not proof of unrestricted RSI. Publisher-indexed abstract was accessible; full-page retrieval was blocked during this request.
3. [Ginkgo Bioworks — autonomous CFPS laboratory interface](https://github.com/ginkgobioworks/ginkgo-automation-cfps). The project describes structured experimental specifications, automated execution and return of results to the AI experimental-design process. Its interface supports the distinction between proposals, validated laboratory protocols and experimental feedback. The example does not establish that the underlying general AI model weights improved during the experiment.
4. [Chen, Wang and Qu — Recursive Self-Improvement in AI: From Bounded Self-Refinement to Autonomous Research Loops, arXiv:2607.07663v2, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07663v2). A **preprint survey**, used only for terminology and to distinguish the system behavior/policy/evaluator/research-process components that may be improved. It is not independent experimental proof of biology-specific RSI.
5. [ACROBiosystems — AIDD-Ready](https://www.acrobiosystems.com/aix/aidd-ready-solutions). Supporting product-workflow context from the preceding research: sample construction/expression, binding and selected functional assays can feed experimental iteration. This is an offering description and does not establish paid AI revenue.
